The network

A guardian for value in motion.

Phylox — from the Greek phýlax, “guardian” — is an independent Layer-1 chain written line by line in Go, not forked from anyone. The design goal is unglamorous and hard: move value quickly, cheaply and predictably, and never lose a coin along the way.

Mechanism

Built like a vault, not a demo.

Four decisions carry the whole design. Each one is boring on purpose — boring is what survives real money.

01

One rhythm: a block every 4 seconds

A steady four-second cadence — fast enough to feel instant at the till, slow enough for nodes across the world to agree before the next block. Finality becomes instant once BFT consensus lands: a sealed block never rolls back.

02

Deflation by use

Half of every block’s fees are burned. The more the network is used, the more supply pressure eases — value flows back to everyone holding, not to a middleman.

03

Guardians, by a ladder

Consensus starts simple and hardens on a planned ladder: a single trusted validator at launch, then a two-thirds BFT vote, then staking when the coin has a real price. The validator set lives in network state, not in a config file.

04

A balance you can prove

State lives in a sparse Merkle tree, so any balance can be proven — present or absent — with a handful of hashes. A phone can verify its own coins without trusting the node it asks. Every hash is domain-separated; nothing can be mistaken for anything else.
